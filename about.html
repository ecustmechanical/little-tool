About

Hi, I'm Marcus Reed — and I started TackleCalc because fishing advice was broken.

I've fished for most of my life: stocked trout behind my parents' house as a kid, smallmouth on clear northern rivers in my twenties, and later bass tournaments and inshore trips for redfish whenever I could escape inland. I'm not a professional guide and I've never held a pro-staff contract. What I am is the guy who reads the spec sheet before he buys a reel, and who keeps a notebook on the boat to write down what actually worked.

TackleCalc grew out of that notebook — and out of a frustration almost every angler knows. Ask one question — “what line should I use for bass?” — and you'll get five answers from five experts. Ask what rod to pair with a reel, and someone will tell you power matters, someone else will say action, and nobody will explain how it all fits together.

The problem isn't that the advice is wrong. It's that it's fragmented. Most species guides are written by many authors over many years, each adding a technique without removing the old one. The result reads like a parts catalog, not a setup. A beginner can't tell which rod, reel and rig were ever meant to work together — because nobody ever said they were.

What this site does differently

Every species on TackleCalc has one rig sheet. It names a rod, a reel, a main line, a leader, hook sizes, the rigs worth tying, the bait worth bringing, and the mistakes worth avoiding — all chosen as a system. When you read a TackleCalc sheet, the rod power, the line class and the hook size are consistent with each other, because they were designed together and tested together.

That's also why I hand-compile the data instead of scraping it. Each sheet follows mainstream regional tackle conventions — the same logic most good pro-shop staff would walk you through — but it's written down, structured and free. When tackle or techniques change, I update the sheet and note what changed. There is no paywall, no sign-up wall and no “download our app to see the answer” wall.

How the site makes money

TackleCalc is free and I intend to keep it that way. The site is supported two ways: display advertising once the library of free tools and guides is large enough to justify it, and — occasionally — affiliate links. If a product link appears next to a rig sheet, it is clearly labeled. If you buy through that link, I may earn a small commission at no extra cost to you. That commission never decides a recommendation; the species and the water decide that.

A note on honesty

Fishing regulations and tackle standards vary by region, so treat every rig sheet as a well-researched starting point, not a legal ruling. Check your local rules before you fish. If a sheet disagrees with something your local pros swear by, they're probably right for your water — and I'd genuinely like to hear about it so the sheet can improve.

Want a species added, a tool built, or a rig sheet corrected? Send me a note. Real fishing questions get real answers.

— Marcus Reed
