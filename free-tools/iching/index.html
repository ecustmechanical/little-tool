How the I Ching works

The I Ching (Book of Changes) uses 64 hexagrams, each consisting of six lines. Each line is either yang (solid ━━━) or yin (broken ━ ━). A hexagram is formed from two trigrams — a lower trigram and an upper trigram — each composed of three lines.

In the traditional coin toss method, three coins are tossed six times to build the hexagram from the bottom up. Each toss produces one line:

  • 3 heads (old yang) — a solid changing line (transforms to yin)
  • 2 heads, 1 tail (young yang) — a stable solid line
  • 1 head, 2 tails (young yin) — a stable broken line
  • 3 tails (old yin) — a broken changing line (transforms to yang)

The Eight Trigrams (八卦)

Each hexagram is built from two of the eight trigrams, which represent fundamental natural forces:

  • ☰ Qian (乾) — Heaven · Creative
  • ☷ Kun (坤) — Earth · Receptive
  • ☳ Zhen (震) — Thunder · Arousing
  • ☵ Kan (坎) — Water · Abysmal
  • ☶ Gen (艮) — Mountain · Stillness
  • ☴ Xun (巽) — Wind · Gentle
  • ☲ Li (离) — Fire · Clinging
  • ☱ Dui (兑) — Lake · Joyous

Frequently asked questions

What is the I Ching?

The I Ching (易经), also known as the Book of Changes, is one of the oldest Chinese classic texts. It consists of 64 hexagrams, each made of six lines that are either yin (broken) or yang (solid), used for divination and philosophical guidance.

How does the coin toss method work?

In the traditional coin toss method, three coins are thrown six times. Each throw produces a line: 3 heads = old yang (changing), 2 heads + 1 tail = young yang (stable), 1 head + 2 tails = young yin (stable), 3 tails = old yin (changing). Lines are built from bottom to top.

What are changing lines?

Changing lines (老阳/老阴) are lines that transform into their opposite — yang becomes yin or yin becomes yang. They indicate areas of special significance and transformation in the reading. This generator marks them with an asterisk.
