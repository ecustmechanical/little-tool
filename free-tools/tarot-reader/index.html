How to read the draw

  • Upright — the card's energy is available and moving. Read it at face value.
  • Reversed — the same energy, but blocked, delayed, internalised or running to excess. It is rarely a simple opposite.
  • Single card — treat it as the theme to sit with, not a yes/no verdict.
  • Past / Present / Future — the first card is what led here, the second is what is active now, the third is the direction of travel if nothing changes. It is a story, not a sentence.

If a card does not seem to fit, that is useful information rather than a failure. The reading is a prompt to look at your situation from an angle you had not tried — you decide what is true.

About the Major Arcana

This reader uses the 22 trump cards of a standard tarot deck, from The Fool (0) through The World (21). They are the deck's big archetypes — beginnings, choices, endings, upheaval, hope — rather than the finer detail of everyday events. That is what makes them a good fit for a short general reading: a single Major Arcana card already carries a full theme.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between an upright and a reversed tarot card?

An upright card is read at face value. A reversed card (drawn upside down) usually points to the same energy being blocked, internalised, delayed or excessive. Reversal does not simply mean the opposite of the upright meaning.

How does a three-card past / present / future spread work?

The first card describes the influences that led to the current situation, the second describes what is active right now, and the third suggests the direction things are moving if nothing changes. It is a narrative, not a fixed prophecy.

Does this tarot reader predict the future?

No. The draw is random and the readings are reflective prompts — a way to look at a situation from an angle you had not considered. Nothing here is a prediction, and nothing is medical, legal or financial advice.

Why only the 22 Major Arcana?

The Major Arcana are the 22 trump cards of a standard tarot deck. They carry the broadest, most archetypal themes — major life lessons rather than day-to-day detail — which makes them the most useful set for a short, general reading.

Is anything I type stored or sent anywhere?

Nothing is stored, and no question text is transmitted. The shuffle runs in your browser with JavaScript, and if you use the optional question field it never leaves the page.
